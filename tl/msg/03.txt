@3001
A Shu-Han officer who served Liu Zhang, then surrendered to Liu Bei. Led units with distinction in the south and the north. After the death of Xiahou Ba, General of Chariots, he shared that rank with Liao Hua as General of Chariots of the Left.

@3002
A Shu-Han retainer. Foreseeing Liu Zhang's defeat, he went over to Liu Bei, and talked Ma Chao, who had come to aid Liu Zhang, into joining Liu Bei. Served in the south and north.

@3003
A Wei officer. Put down Guanqiu Jian's revolt. In Zhuge Dan's rebellion he led the vanguard of the loyal army, beat Zhu Yi and Tang Zi, and killed Yu Quan. Said to be versed in the Book of Changes. 2nd General of the South.

@3004
A fierce officer under Cao Zhen who wielded a 60-pound glaive and a meteor hammer. Fought Shu-Han at Chencang, slew Xie Xiong and badly wounded Zhang Ni. He gave chase, but was cut down by Wei Yan.

@3005
A Wei officer who led a unit as Marshal of the Army under Cao Zhen and Sima Yi. Later Inspector of Yongzhou, he hunted Xiahou Ba when Xiahou Ba rebelled against Wei. At Mount Tielong he rescued Sima Zhao.

@3006
A Wei officer, Xiahou Dun's nephew. Captured at Mount Dingjun, he came back to Wei in a hostage exchange for Chen Shi. Later attacked Wu on three fronts under Cao Zhen, but was beaten by Lu Xun's army.

@3007
A Jin officer. Put down Zhuge Dan's rebellion. Joined the war on Wu as 4th General of the South. Opposed Du Yu's plan for a swift advance, but was told: "We must use our momentum, like splitting bamboo."

@3008
First emperor of Jin (Emperor Wu), Sima Zhao's eldest son. Forced the Wei emperor Cao Huan to abdicate and took the throne. On the advice of Yang Hu and Du Yu he conquered Wu, made Sun Hao surrender and united the land.

@3009
Wu's General of the Front, Sun Kuang's grandson. Guarding Xiakou, he fled to Jin in fear of the cruel Sun Hao and was made General of Cavalry. When Wu fell he wept: "Oh Heaven, whose crime is this?"

@3010
A Wu officer. Led a relief army to aid Zhuge Dan's rebellion in Wei. The Wei general Wang Ji urged him to surrender, but he kept faith and died fighting.

@3011
A Wu officer who, with Zhou Tai, joined the renowned Sun Ce. Led the vanguard into Nan commandery but was badly beaten and nearly executed. In the war on Guan Yu he led the fleet that surrounded him.

@3012
A Wu retainer, Wu's last Prime Minister. Fought hard against Jin's great army. Urged by Shen Ying and others to surrender, he refused, saying he would "die with the realm", and charged into the melee to his death.

@3013
A Wu officer, granted the Sun surname by Sun Ce. At Yiling he lost his officers Li Yi and Xie Jing. Besieged in Yiling castle by Shu, he held firm until Lu Xun relieved him.

@3014
A Wu officer, Sun Jian's third son and Protector of Danyang. Brave like his brother Sun Ce, but violent; he flogged his men when drunk, and they murdered him at a banquet.

@3015
Wu's second emperor, Sun Quan's third son (seventh in history). A bright ruler, he plotted to kill the tyrannical Sun Chen but failed; he was deposed and reduced to Prince of Kuaiji.

@3016
A Shu-Han officer who fought well in Zhuge Liang's southern and northern campaigns, and later died trying to rescue Jiang Wei. In history he subdued the tribes, won their trust and worked to keep the region at peace.

@3017
An officer under Liu Zhang who shot dead Liu Bei's advisor Pang Tong at Fallen Phoenix Slope. Captured, he refused Liu Bei's offer: "A loyal subject serves no second lord." He was beheaded with dignity.

@3018
A Shu-Han officer, Zhao Yun's eldest son. Told Zhuge Liang, then Liu Shan, of his father's death, and was made Commander of the Tiger Guard. Guarded Mount Jinping, where his father was buried, with his brother Zhao Guang.

@3019
Later Han's General Who Calms the Waves, Chen Gui's son. Became Liu Bei's man when Liu Bei took over Xuzhou from Tao Qian. When Liu Bei was crushed by Cao Cao, he went over to Cao Cao and was made an Protector.

@3020
A Wu officer of strange looks: yellow face, red eyes. Joined Sun Ce during the war with Liu Yao. At Red Cliffs he was the fourth commander. Slain by Pang De at Ruxu.

@3021
A Wu officer who introduced Yu Fan and Hua Tuo to Sun Ce. Fought well defending Ruxu, but drowned when a gale overturned his ship; Sun Quan had his body found and buried.

@3022
A Shu-Han officer. Lost a duel to Lady Zhurong in the south. Later, in the northern campaigns, he led units in ambushes and diversions. General of Inner Spirit.

@3023
A Wu officer. Captured Guan Yu with Zhu Ran and was rewarded with Guan Yu's crescent blade. Pursued by Guan Yu's son Guan Xing, he met Guan Yu's ghost, and was cut down in his fright.

@3024
A Wei officer, Inspector of Yangzhou. Enraged by Sima Shi's tyranny in deposing Cao Fang, he rebelled with Guanqiu Jian. Beaten, he fled to Wu, but his ally Zhuge Dan distrusted him and had him killed.

@3025
A Wu retainer. In the taking of Jing Province he read the moves of Guan Yu and Cao Ren exactly and won the day for Wu. Made General of Cavalry when Sun Quan took the throne, and later Prime Minister.

@3026
A Shu-Han retainer who served in the northern campaigns as Chief Clerk. At odds with Wei Yan, he clashed with him after Zhuge Liang's death, one cause of Wei Yan's revolt. Bitter over his rank, he spoke rashly and was made a commoner.

@3027
An officer under Liu Zhang. Held off Liu Bei's army with Wu Yi and others, but was surrounded and surrendered. Guarding Baxi, he fought the Wei general Zhang He well, but an ambush cut off his retreat and he died.

@3028
A Wu officer who fought with a gilded axe. Served under Sun Huan against Shu-Han. As he raised his axe over the fallen Zhang Bao, Guan Xing struck off his head from behind.

@3029
A Wu officer. Fighting Shu-Han, he shot Zhang Bao's horse to save his comrade Li Yi and brought Zhang Bao down. The next day Guan Xing took him alive, and he was killed in the Shu-Han camp.

@3030
A Wu retainer, Minister of Ceremonies. Plotted with Sun Jun to kill the overbearing Zhuge Ke, and was made Grand Marshal for it. He then plotted against the tyrannical Sun Chen, but was killed by him.

@3031
A Shu-Han retainer, a clerk of Yongchang. Held Yongchang to the last against Yong Kai and others. Gave Zhuge Liang, on his way south, a war map called the "Map of the South", and was made Instructor of the Army.

@3032
A Wu retainer. Served on Yuan Shu's staff, gave up on him and joined Sun Ce. Met Liu Bei as the envoy arranging his marriage to Sun Quan's sister.

@3033
An officer under Yuan Shang, Lu Xiang's elder brother. Surrendered to Yuan Tan and took a general's seal, but sent it to Cao Cao and changed sides. Attacked Xinye with Lu Xiang, and was speared by the brave Zhao Yun.

@3034
A retainer of Wei and Jin, Jia Kui's son and Sima Zhao's confidant. Directed the killing of the Wei emperor Cao Mao. After Zhong Hui's revolt he went to Shu and restored order in Chengdu. Was reluctant to attack Wu.

@3035
An officer under Dong Zhuo. After Dong Zhuo's death he raided Chang'an on Jia Xu's advice, killed Wang Yun and others, and ruled the court with Li Jue. Became a bandit and was killed by his man Wu Xi.

@3036
An officer under Liu Du. Faced Liu Bei's army, but was no match for Zhang Fei and Zhao Yun and surrendered. Released, he attacked again, and one blow from Zhao Yun knocked him from his horse.

@3037
A Wei officer, Xiahou Yuan's second son. On Sima Yi's recommendation he led the vanguard against Shu-Han with his brother Xiahou Ba. Later fought against Gongsun Yuan.

@3038
An officer under Yuan Shao. At Guandu he fought Cao Cao's fierce warrior Xu Chu. Later joined Cao Cao with his comrade Zhang He. Attacked Liu Bei's army at Runan and slew Liu Pi.

@3039
A Wei officer, 1st General of the East. Led the vanguard against Gongsun Yuan. In Guanqiu Jian's revolt he led troops with Deng Ai and Wang Ji, beat Guanqiu Jian and pacified Huainan.

@3040
A Shu-Han officer, Zhao Yun's second son. Told Zhuge Liang of his father's death with his brother Zhao Tong, and guarded his tomb as a Gate General. In history he was posted at Tazhong under Jiang Wei and died at the front.

@3041
A Shu-Han retainer, once Yuan Shao's man. While Liu Bei was Yuan Shao's guest, he carried Liu Bei's letter to Guan Yu. When Liu Bei became Governor of Jingzhou, he was called to serve as an aide.

@3042
A Shu-Han retainer, one of the many officials who urged Liu Bei to take the throne. Made an Academician at Zhuge Liang's first northern campaign, he handled government, and also tutored the emperor Liu Shan.

@3043

@3044

@3045

@3047
A Shu-Han retainer, Palace Attendant. In the "Memorial on Dispatching the Troops", Zhuge Liang named him, with Dong Yun and Fei Yi, as sincere and loyal, and gave him charge of all palace affairs.

@3048

@3050

@3051

@3052

@3054

@3055

@3056

@3057

@3059
